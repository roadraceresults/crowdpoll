UI/UX Revisions Architecture

Nine revisions to CrowdPoll's presentation layer — and, since the last artifact, one behavioural mechanism underneath them. The requirements grew from 117 lines to 290, and the growth is two new sections: a session snapshot shared across a page and broadcast between windows, and a landing state for a verification link that arrives with no session. Between them they close the largest open question the previous run raised. One of them cannot work as written.

01 · ScopeNine revisions, and the mechanism that arrived with two of them

Seven of these revisions move markup, CSS and client rendering; none of them needs the server. The two that arrived since the last artifact — session propagation and the verification landing — are different in kind. They describe runtime behaviour rather than appearance, they answer a question the previous run could only raise, and one of them requires an edit to a file the requirements do not mention.

02 · ComponentsWhere the nine revisions land

Eight of the nine revisions are confined to src/public. The ninth reaches the server — not through an API, which the requirements correctly rule out, but through the page route that decides whether profile.html is sent at all.

The claim that no API changes is worth stating plainly, because it is true and it is load-bearing: /api/me is unchanged, both verify routes are unchanged, and postman/openapi.json needs no edit for any of R1–R7 or R9. R8's cost is a page route, not an endpoint — but contracts/page-routes.md documents that route's behaviour, and tests/pages.test.ts asserts it.

03 · Card shellA sibling of the poll card, not a subclass of it

D1 is precise about provenance, and the precision matters. The three pages take pollCard.css's tokens and four of its rules, and they take none of PollCard's behaviour — no rear face, no flip, no polling timer, no applyPoll. Reusing POLL_CARD_TEMPLATE would have imported all of it.

The three anatomies

All three cards use the same four regions in the same order. What differs is what the body holds and how many actions hang off it — and, on Profile, that the subtitle slot carries the avatar rather than text.

Two sentences in tension“A single card body with no slack” and min-height:640px are both requirements of the Profile card. A fixed minimum produces slack whenever the content is shorter than it — which, at three field groups, it will be on a wide viewport. Recorded as INC‑6.

04 · StylesheetsWhat D2 moves, and where it lands

D2 is stated as a swap: the three card pages stop loading styles.css and start loading what index.html loads, with anything they still need promoted into site.css. Read against the working tree, the swap has a consequence the directive does not mention — four other pages keep loading styles.css, so every promoted rule ends up present twice on those pages.

The promotion is one-way, the loading is notEvery rule moved out of styles.css into site.css keeps applying to the four pages that load both sheets — from a different file, at a different position in the cascade. If a rule is copied the two can drift; if it is moved, its selector now reaches index.html and every admin page, which previously never saw it. Neither outcome is stated. Recorded as UNS‑2 and UNS‑3, and as breaking change BC‑2.

05 · HeaderOne element moves; the layout follows on its own

R3 is careful to specify a mechanism rather than an outcome: move .header-nav after .header-search in SITE_HEADER_MARKUP, add no spacer, no margin-left:auto, and leave justify-content:space-between alone. That works because .header-search already carries flex:1 1 auto and max-width:640px in site.css — it is the only flexible child, so it is the only thing that can absorb the slack.

Two things the requirement does not reach. The header's signed-out state puts a Log In and a Create Account button in #header-actions, and R3 describes only “the user name and avatar at the far right”.

And the source-order mechanism stops applying below 768 px. site.css gives .header-search order:3, flex:1 1 100% and max-width:none in its max-width:767.98px block, which drops the search onto its own line after every other child — explicitly overriding source order. The good news is that the outcome is unchanged: brand, Create Poll, actions, then search, both before and after R3. The reorder is a no-op on narrow viewports, and the requirement neither says so nor says it should be. Recorded as UNS‑13 and UNS‑14.

06 · ParticipantsThree visibilities, two sources, one sort

R1's problem statement is exact: today an admin looking at an invitation-only poll sees who was invited and an admin looking at a public poll sees who voted, and neither can see which invited people have voted. The fix needs the voted set in a branch that has never loaded it — adminPoll.js fetches it only when the poll is not invitation-only.

Two questions the picture raises and the requirements do not answer. A roster row already renders a second piece of text beside the address — the voter's display name — and carries a Ban from Voting control; whether the suffix joins the address, replaces the display name, or occupies its own slot decides what that row looks like. And “sorted by email address” does not say whether the comparison folds case. Recorded as AMB‑9, AMB‑2 and AMB‑3. R2 renames the invite-row button from Add to Invite and raises nothing.

07 · SessionOne snapshot per page, and how it reaches the other window

The problem R7 names is concrete and currently real: app.js fetches /api/me once on DOMContentLoaded, and pollGrid.js captures that result and closes over it in isSignedIn. A window that was open when the session changed elsewhere stays as it was — a logged-out header, guest-gated cards, a stale email address — until someone reloads it. The fix is a single owned value plus two ways of learning it changed.

Adoption re-renders; it does not navigate — with one exception the requirement states explicitly. Login and Create Account exist to obtain a session, so on null → signed-in they navigate to the home page. In practice this fires rarely, because D3 already navigates those cards home when the link request returns 200; it is the window that was left sitting on the form that it rescues.

Same browser: the broadcast closes it immediately

Where BroadcastChannel is unavailable the snapshot goes to a single localStorage key and the page listens for storage; the payload, the comparison and the re-render are identical. That fallback stores the user's email address on the device, and the requirement sets no lifetime for it — recorded as AMB‑8 and raised as CL‑2.

08 · LandingThe window that consumed the link, and the guard in its way

R7 keeps the requesting window current. R8 handles the other half: the window the link opened, which for an email-change verification may hold no session at all. /api/me/email-change/verify requires none — it consumes the token, updates the address and redirects — so a link opened in a mail client's in-app browser succeeds and lands somewhere that cannot show what happened.

The guard R8 does not name

R8 locates the redirect in profile.html, which does send a session-less visitor to /login, and requires the page to render the result before it evaluates the session. But pages.ts makes the same decision one layer earlier, and profile.html is never sent at all. R8 also states that no endpoint is added and neither verify route changes — both true, and both beside the point. The change it needs is to a page route.

QueryTitleSubtitleAction
changed=1Email Address ChangedYour email address has been updated. Sign in with your new address to continue.Log In → /login
error=invalid-linkLink No Longer ValidExpired, already used, replaced by a newer request, or the address is now in use — the four causes stated together.Log In → /login

Two properties of this card are deliberate and worth preserving through the spec. It names no email address — the window cannot obtain one, and R8 forbids passing it through the redirect to be reflected back into the page. And its wording describes the link's outcome rather than the window's session, so it stays accurate when the browser happens to hold a session for a different account.

09 · NavigationDeclared destinations only

D5 settles a question that would otherwise recur on every new control: where does “back” go? Every navigation this feature adds is an anchor with a literal href, or a location.href assignment. Nothing consults browser history, so no control's destination depends on how the user arrived.

10 · DecisionsPositions this architecture takes

Six decisions the requirements leave open, resolved here so the spec has somewhere to start. Each is reversible by a one-line answer from the product owner; the clarifications in section 13 are the questions that would reverse them.

ADR‑1The /profile guard is relaxed narrowly, not removed

pages.ts serves profile.html to a session-less request only when changed=1 or error=invalid-link is present. Every other session-less request keeps redirecting to /login, so FR‑014 holds everywhere the result card is not involved.

The alternative — making /profile public and letting the client decide — is simpler and rejected: it turns a guarded route into an unguarded one for the sake of two query strings, and the page's own JavaScript would become the only thing standing between a guest and a profile editor.

Because the narrow form buys the whole feature at the cost of one condition, and the broad form spends a guard to buy nothing extra.

ADR‑2The snapshot is owned by app.js and read through it

app.js holds the value, exposes a getter and a subscribe function on the existing client namespace, and is the only module that calls /api/me. pollGrid.js's isSignedIn becomes a read of that value rather than a closure over one captured in init; the three card pages subscribe rather than fetch.

Without this, the grid's gating and the header can disagree inside one page — which is today's behaviour, and is the second orphan the feature has to remove.

Because two fetches of the same fact is how the two halves of a page drift apart.

ADR‑3The shell is one small module plus rules in site.css

No fourth stylesheet. The shell's markup helper ships as a client module the three pages load, and its rules join site.css — the sheet D2 already nominates as the destination for promoted rules.

Because a new sheet would need adding to every page that loads the shell, and D2 has already chosen where shared page-level rules live.

ADR‑4Promoted rules are moved, not copied

Rules the card pages still need leave styles.css and land in site.css once. The four admin pages load both sheets, so a copy would give them two definitions that can drift; a move gives them one, at a new cascade position.

The cost is that a moved selector now also reaches index.html and the admin pages, which never saw it from styles.css… except that they did, because they load styles.css too. The only page whose exposure genuinely widens is index.html, which loads site.css and not styles.css. That page is the one to check visually after the migration.

Because one definition that moves is auditable; two that agree today are not.

ADR‑5The label is a suffix on the address, not a column

R1 says the address “will be followed by the text”, so the suffix joins the address in the row's first slot: addr - Invited - Voted. The row's existing display-name slot and its Ban from Voting control keep their positions.

An earlier draft of these requirements asked for a separate status column, and the architecture that answered it built a three-track grid. The current text asks for something smaller, and gets a smaller change.

Because “followed by” is a statement about one row, and a column is a statement about all of them.

ADR‑6The localStorage fallback is cleared when the snapshot is null

The fallback stores the whole snapshot, including the user's email address. Writing null on sign-out removes it rather than leaving the last signed-in identity readable on a shared device, and it is also what makes the fallback deliver a sign-out to other tabs at all.

Because a cache of an identity that outlives the session is a liability, and clearing it is also the correct signal.

11 · AssumptionsDefaults taken where the text is silent

  • A‑1

    A row that is both invited and voted carries both suffixes, in that order: addr - Invited - Voted. Replacement would lose the fact that the address was invited, which is the column's original purpose.

  • A‑2

    Voter rows keep the display name they render today; the - Voted suffix joins the address, not the name.

  • A‑3

    The roster sort compares email addresses case-insensitively. Addresses are stored lower-cased on the server, so the two comparisons agree in practice; the folded one also survives a draft address typed in mixed case.

  • A‑4

    The Banned Voter List keeps its current unlabelled rendering and its own sort. A ban removes the address from the invited list and drops its vote, so both suffixes would be false on every banned row.

  • A‑5

    The close control is an anchor to / carrying an × glyph in the poll card's .icon-btn chrome, with an accessible name of “Close”.

  • A‑6

    The three cards take a max-width in the region of the poll card's own width and centre horizontally; if a card outgrows a short viewport the page scrolls, and the card does not become a scroll container.

  • A‑7

    “Send Verification Link” follows D3 like every other submission, treating the endpoint's 202 as the success case and staying on the card for 429 and 400.

  • A‑8

    Each card carries one status line per action, below the action it belongs to, and page-load notices such as /login?error=invalid-link render in the card's first status line.

  • A‑9

    The oauth provider-button branch in login.html is carried into the shell unchanged and stays hidden in native mode, consistent with the Exclusions section deferring all oauth work.

  • A‑10

    An adopted snapshot re-renders the Profile card the same way whatever its source — a broadcast clears the Change Email Address box exactly as a visibility re-check does.

12 · FindingsThirty, of which one is structural

Read against the working tree at generation time. Inconsistencies are contradictions inside 013 itself; ambiguities admit more than one reading; unspecified aspects are details the text never reaches. Differences between 013 and the current implementation are not defects — they are the feature — and appear as breaking changes at the end.

Inconsistencies

INC‑1

The verification landing cannot be reached by the change it specifies

R8 states that “no new endpoint is added, and neither verify route changes”, and locates the redirect it must remove in profile.html. That redirect exists, but it is the second one. pages.ts redirects any session-less request for /profile to /login before profile.html is sent, so the result card is unreachable in exactly the case — a link opened in a browser with no session — that R8 exists to serve.

The claim about endpoints and verify routes is true and does not help: what has to change is a page route, which is neither.

taken ADR‑1 — relax the guard for result parameters only. See CL‑1, Figure 10, BC‑1.
INC‑2

Create Account navigates both on click and on response

R5 says “when the ‘Create Account’ button is clicked, the user will be navigated to the home page”. Four lines later D3 says submissions await the response, and that 429 or 400 stays on the card and renders the reason. Both cannot hold on a rate-limited submission.

taken D3 governs; the click-time sentence is read as shorthand for the success case. Same reading as R4, whose text has the same shape.
INC‑3

Send Verification Link navigates unconditionally, and its endpoint does not return 200

R6 says the click navigates home, with no await and no failure path — while the paragraph directly below it applies the await-then-branch rule to Save User Name. Compounding it, POST /api/me/email-change returns 202, so a rule phrased as “200 navigates” would not fire for this button even if it were applied.

taken A‑7 — treat 202 as the success case and follow D3. See CL‑6.
INC‑4

Two new requirements, three visibilities

R1 opens by saying “there are two new requirements, one for ‘PRIVATE - INVITATION ONLY’ polls and one for ‘PUBLIC’ polls”, then specifies three — the third being PRIVATE - ANYONE WITH THE LINK. The count is stale, not the content.

taken All three branches are specified and implemented. Editorial fix.
INC‑5

create-profile.html does not exist

The stylesheet-migration paragraph names login.html, create-profile.html and profile.html — twice, once under R4 and once under R5. The page R5 otherwise describes is create-account.html.

taken Read as create-account.html throughout. Editorial fix.
INC‑6

The Profile card is both slack-free and 640 px tall

R6 asks for “a single card body with no slack” and for min-height:640px. At three field groups the content will be shorter than 640 px on a wide viewport, and the difference is slack.

taken The minimum wins where they conflict; “no slack” is read as “no rear face and no filler regions”, which is what it means on the other two cards.

Ambiguities

AMB‑1

Whether an invited voter carries one suffix or two

“Followed by the text ‘ - Invited’ for every row. And then the text ‘ - Voted’ if they have exercised their vote” reads as concatenation, but replacement is a defensible reading of the same two sentences.

taken A‑1 — both, in order. See CL‑3.
AMB‑2

Whether the display name survives on voter rows

Voter rows currently render the voter's userName beside the address. R1 describes only the address and its suffix.

taken A‑2 — the name stays. See CL‑5.
AMB‑3

The collation of the email sort

“Sorted by email address” does not say whether the comparison folds case or is locale-aware. Invited rows currently use a bare .sort(); voter rows use localeCompare on the display name.

taken A‑3 — case-insensitive. See CL‑10.
AMB‑4

What the ‘X’ control is

“An ‘X’ icon … rendered in the style of the ‘Back arrow’ on the back-side of the poll card” names a glyph and a style from a different glyph. And “back arrow” refers to two different elements in this document: the poll card's .icon-btn.back-btn here, and the edit page's .admin-back-link in R9.

taken A‑5 — the card's icon-button chrome, carrying an ×.
AMB‑5

Whether a broadcast clears the Change Email Address box

R7 specifies the clearing for “a re-check that changes the snapshot”. Nothing elsewhere distinguishes a re-check from an adopted broadcast, and the reason for clearing — the pending link has been superseded — applies identically to both.

taken A‑10 — any adopted snapshot clears it.
AMB‑6

Card width, and what happens on a short viewport

“Sized appropriately to fit the contents” and “centered horizontally” fix no width. Poll cards take theirs from the grid, which these cards do not join. The Profile card's 640 px minimum exceeds a phone's viewport height.

taken A‑6 — a max-width near the poll card's, and the page scrolls rather than the card.
AMB‑7

The oauth branch inside a page being rebuilt

The Exclusions section defers all ENV_SIGNIN_METHOD=oauth work, and R4 rebuilds the page that currently contains the provider-button branch. Whether the shell keeps it, hides it, or drops it is not stated.

taken A‑9 — carried across unchanged and hidden in native mode. See CL‑7.
AMB‑8

The localStorage fallback's key and lifetime

R7 specifies “a single localStorage key” without naming it or saying when it is removed. The stored payload is the whole snapshot, which contains the user's email address.

taken ADR‑6 — cleared when the snapshot becomes null. See CL‑2.
AMB‑9

Where the suffix sits in a row that already has three parts

A roster row renders an address, an optional display name, and a Ban from Voting control. “The email address will be followed by the text” does not say whether the suffix joins the address text, takes the display name's slot, or opens a new one — which decides whether the Ban control moves.

taken ADR‑5 — the suffix joins the address; nothing else moves. See CL‑4.
AMB‑10

Where a refusal renders on each card

D3 requires 429 and 400 to “render the reason” on the card, but the shell's regions are a title row, a subtitle, a body and an action row — none of them a status line. Profile has two independent actions and therefore needs two. Page-load notices such as /login?error=invalid-link belong to no action at all.

taken A‑8 — one status line per action, page-load notices in the first. See CL‑8.

Unspecified aspects

UNS‑1

Where the card shell lives

D1 mandates extraction but names no module, no file and no loading order for the three pages.

taken ADR‑3. See CL‑9.
UNS‑2

Which rules move out of styles.css

“Whatever those pages still need from it — the .error colour, the field styles the card body wants” is an example, not a list. The migration is the largest regression surface in the feature.

taken Enumerate by auditing the three pages against styles.css during /speckit-plan; the list belongs in the plan, not the requirements.
UNS‑3

Whether promoted rules are moved or copied

Four pages keep loading styles.css as well as site.css. A copy leaves two definitions to drift; a move changes the cascade position on those pages and newly exposes the rule to index.html.

taken ADR‑4 — moved. Check index.html visually after.
UNS‑4

Whether sign-out propagates

Logout navigates its own window to /, and the reloaded page will fetch and broadcast null — so other windows do drop to the guest state, as a side effect. R7 never states it as a requirement, so nothing would catch it regressing.

taken Falls out of D6/D7; worth an explicit acceptance criterion in the spec.
UNS‑5

status is compared but never rendered

It is in the comparison tuple alongside five fields the pages do render. Either something is meant to depend on it, or comparing it only causes re-renders nothing can show.

taken Keep it in the comparison — a spurious re-render is cheap, a missed one is not.
UNS‑6

The double fetch on an ordinary load

R7 re-checks on pageshow only when event.persisted is true, which avoids this — but the ordering against the load fetch, and whether a re-check may overtake it, is not stated.

taken One in-flight fetch at a time; a re-check while one is outstanding is dropped.
UNS‑7

How the result card announces itself

The pages it replaces announce through <p role="status">. The result card is a whole page state arriving at load, and R8 says nothing about assistive technology.

taken Rendered at load in the document, not injected into a live region — it is the page, not an update to it.
UNS‑8

The close control's name and focus order

Three cards gain an icon-only control whose accessible name, position in the tab order, and keyboard behaviour are unstated.

taken A‑5 — an anchor named “Close”, first in the card's tab order because it is first in the markup.
UNS‑9

Whether the admin pages' stylesheet set changes

D2 speaks only of the three card pages. The four admin pages keep styles.css by omission rather than by decision.

taken Unchanged — they are outside this feature.
UNS‑10

Contract documents for the changed page route

D4's “no change to postman/openapi.json” is scoped to the roster labels. contracts/page-routes.md documents the /profile guard that ADR‑1 changes, and nothing says it must be updated.

taken Update contracts/page-routes.md; postman/openapi.json genuinely needs nothing.
UNS‑11

Test impact is not acknowledged

tests/pages.test.ts asserts the page-route guards; several suites assert markup on the rebuilt pages. 013 mentions no test at all.

taken Enumerate affected suites in /speckit-tasks; at minimum the /profile guard assertions change.
UNS‑12

The Banned Voter List

R1 re-keys “the Participants list” to the email address and adds suffixes. The banned list sits in the same section, renders the same row shape, and is not mentioned.

taken A‑4 — unlabelled, and left on its own sort. See CL‑4.
UNS‑13

The header's signed-out far right

R3 describes the far right as “the user name and avatar”. Signed out, that slot holds a Log In and a Create Account button, and the requirement does not say the arrangement is meant to hold for guests too.

taken The same arrangement; only the contents of #header-actions differ, as today.
UNS‑14

Source order stops governing below 768 px

site.css gives .header-search order:3 under max-width:767.98px, explicitly overriding source order. R3's mechanism therefore applies only to wide viewports — harmlessly, since the narrow result is identical before and after, but the requirement neither notices nor sanctions it.

taken Leave the media block alone; record the no-op so a later reader does not “fix” it.

13 · ClarificationsTen questions, in the order they change the work

Each has a default recorded in section 11, so none of them blocks drafting. The first three change what gets built rather than how it is described, and CL‑1 changes whether R8 works at all.

CL‑1

Does /profile become servable without a session — and how widely?

Only when changed=1 or error=invalid-link is present, which keeps FR‑014 everywhere else? Or for every request, letting the page decide? R8 assumes neither, because it does not know the guard is there.

default ADR‑1 — the narrow relaxation. scope & security
CL‑2

Is the localStorage fallback cleared on sign-out?

It holds the snapshot, including the email address, on the device. Clearing it is also what delivers a sign-out to other tabs when BroadcastChannel is unavailable.

default ADR‑6 — cleared. privacy
CL‑3

Does an invited voter's row read - Invited - Voted, or does - Voted replace - Invited?

The whole point of R1 is letting an admin see which invitees have voted; both readings do that, but they answer “who was invited and has not voted” differently at a glance.

default A‑1 — both suffixes. user experience
CL‑4

Does the suffix join the address, and does the Ban control stay where it is?

And does the Banned Voter List, which shares the row shape, take the labels and the new sort or keep what it has?

default ADR‑5 and A‑4 — the suffix joins the address; nothing else moves; banned rows stay unlabelled. user experience
CL‑5

Does the voter's display name still render on public and link-only rows?

It renders today. R1 describes only the address and its suffix, and re-keys the sort away from the name.

default A‑2 — it stays. user experience
CL‑6

Does Send Verification Link navigate on click, or await the response?

If it awaits, the success status is 202, not 200 — and D3 needs re-wording to say so, or to say “a successful response” instead of naming a code.

default A‑7 — await, treating 202 as success. user experience
CL‑7

What do the three rebuilt cards do in oauth mode?

The Exclusions section defers oauth work, but R4 rebuilds the page that currently branches on it. Carrying the branch across costs little; dropping it means restoring it later inside a shell that never held it.

default A‑9 — carried across unchanged. scope
CL‑8

Where does a card render a refusal, and where does a page-load notice go?

D3 requires a reason to render on the card without giving the shell a region for it. Profile has two actions and needs two; /login?error=invalid-link belongs to no action.

default A‑8 — one status line per action, notices in the first. user experience
CL‑9

Where does the card shell live, and does it add a stylesheet?
